Porodo TrackFit Neo Smart Health Tracking Band Price in Kenya, Full Specifications and Review (2026)

The Porodo TrackFit Neo Smart Health Tracking Band price in Kenya is KSh 7,500. It is a screenless wearable: no display, just a sensor module on a 25mm band that collects health data and sends it to a phone app. On board are an ECG sensor, heart rate, heart rate variability (HRV), sleep tracking and a contact temperature sensor. It also gives AI-assisted health insights, supports more than 100 sports modes and vibrates for call and notification alerts. A 125mAh battery is rated for up to 10 days of use, magnetic charging takes about 1.5 hours, and it is water resistant.

The screenless design is the defining choice. Without a screen there is nothing to tap, no watch faces and no glancing at the time. The appeal is a discreet band that sits on the wrist all day and night, collects data, and leaves analysis to the phone. That suits people who want health data without another screen competing for attention, but it means the time, notifications and every reading must be checked on the phone.

Buyers should approach the extra “estimated” readings with caution. The band claims estimates of blood glucose, uric acid, blood lipids and body fat. No wrist-worn optical sensor can reliably measure blood chemistry through the skin, and the source itself says these readings are not substitutes for clinical testing. They are best ignored for any medical decision.

The closest alternative at Phonesstore Kenya is Porodo’s own TrackFit with Extra Strap, also KSh 7,500. That band lists blood oxygen (SpO2) tracking, 120+ sports modes, weather, Bluetooth calling and up to 30 days of battery life. The Neo counters with an ECG sensor, HRV tracking, a temperature sensor and the screenless form.

Design and Screenless Concept

The TrackFit Neo has a zinc alloy frame around an ABS and polycarbonate (PC) body on a 25mm band. Zinc alloy gives the module a solid, metallic feel and protects the sensors, while ABS and PC are light, tough plastics. Weight is not specified, but with no screen or large battery the band should be light enough to forget about during sleep, which matters because sleep and HRV data depend on wearing it all night.

The lack of a screen has real consequences. The band cannot show the time, step count or heart rate on the wrist. All readings must be checked in the phone app. Call and message “alerts” are vibrations only, so the wearer knows something has arrived but must take out the phone to see what. Without a display the band also uses less power and is less distracting.

The source does not say how workouts are started on a screenless band: through the app, by automatic detection or by tapping the band. Buyers who want to start and stop workouts from the wrist should confirm how this works.

Health Tracking: ECG, Heart Rate and HRV

The ECG sensor is the Neo’s most advanced feature. An electrocardiogram records the heart’s electrical activity, not just its rate. On wearables this is usually a single-lead ECG: the wearer touches an electrode on the band with a finger of the opposite hand, completing a circuit across the chest for about 30 seconds. A single-lead ECG can reveal irregular rhythms such as atrial fibrillation, but it is far more limited than a hospital’s 12-lead ECG. The source does not say whether the Neo’s ECG has any medical certification or rhythm analysis. Anyone who sees a worrying reading or feels symptoms should see a doctor rather than rely on the band.

Continuous heart rate uses an optical sensor: green LEDs shine into the skin and a detector measures changes in light as blood pulses through the wrist. Accuracy depends on a snug fit and drops during vigorous wrist movement.

Heart rate variability measures the small variations in time between heartbeats. A healthy, rested body varies these intervals more; stress, illness, alcohol, poor sleep and hard training usually reduce HRV. It is most useful as a trend measured overnight, compared against the wearer’s own baseline rather than other people’s numbers. This makes it a good indicator of recovery and readiness, and a big reason serious trainers use screenless bands.

Sleep quality tracking combines movement and heart rate data to estimate time asleep and restlessness. Whether it breaks sleep into stages is not specified. The contact temperature sensor appears in the spec table but its features are not described. Skin temperature trends can flag illness or changes in the body, but skin temperature differs from core body temperature and is not a thermometer replacement. Blood oxygen (SpO2) monitoring is not listed.

Estimated Readings and AI Insights

The Neo claims estimated readings for blood glucose, uric acid, blood lipids, BMI and body fat. These need to be treated very differently.

Blood glucose, uric acid and blood lipids are chemical concentrations in the blood. Measuring them accurately requires a blood sample, or for glucose a sensor inserted under the skin. No wrist-worn optical sensor has been shown to measure them reliably through the skin. Any number the band shows is a statistical estimate, not a measurement. People with diabetes, gout or high cholesterol must keep using a glucometer, laboratory tests and their doctor’s advice, and should never change medication based on these readings. The source itself warns that they are not substitutes for clinical testing.

BMI (body mass index) is simply calculated from the height and weight entered in the app, so it is accurate only if those numbers are, and it says nothing about body composition. Body fat estimates from a wrist band are rough at best.

AI-assisted insights summarise the data into suggestions. These can help spot trends such as poor sleep after late nights, but their quality depends on the reliability of the underlying data.

Fitness, Notifications and Connectivity

More than 100 sports modes cover walking, running, cycling, gym sessions, yoga and many other activities. Mode-specific tracking adjusts how heart rate and movement are interpreted for each sport. GPS is not listed, so route maps for runs and rides need the phone to come along.

The band connects over Bluetooth 5.4, an efficient current standard, and works with Android 5.0 or later and iOS 9.0 or later. That covers virtually any phone in use in Kenya, including older budget Android models. Integration with Google Health is listed as another way to view the data, but the source does not explain what is shared or how.

Battery and Water Resistance

The 125mAh battery is rated for up to 10 days of use and up to 20 days on standby, and magnetic charging takes about 1.5 hours. Heavy use, such as frequent ECG readings or continuous heart rate tracking, will shorten that. The comparison TrackFit lists up to 30 days from a 110mAh battery. The source does not explain the gap, but more frequent sensor sampling for HRV and temperature would be one likely reason. Ten days still means charging only two or three times a month, and a short top-up while getting ready in the morning keeps it going.

Porodo lists water resistance in two ways. IPX8 means the band has passed continuous immersion beyond 1 metre, under conditions the manufacturer sets; the X means it was not rated for dust. 5ATM is a watch-industry rating meaning it withstands pressure equivalent to 50 metres of depth, generally suitable for surface swimming and showers but not diving or water sports at speed. Either way, the band handles sweat, rain and handwashing. Hot water, saunas and seawater are best avoided, and rinsing after salt water protects the seals.

Pros and Cons

The Porodo TrackFit Neo Smart Health Tracking Band has genuine strengths at KSh 7,500. It includes an ECG sensor, continuous heart rate, HRV and sleep tracking, and a contact temperature sensor. It supports 100+ sports modes and gives AI-assisted health insights. The screenless design is discreet and distraction-free, with vibration alerts for calls and notifications. It connects over Bluetooth 5.4 with wide compatibility and integrates with Google Health. Battery life is up to 10 days, with 1.5-hour magnetic charging. The build uses a zinc alloy frame and it carries IPX8 / 5ATM water resistance.

Its main limitations are that it cannot show the time, readings or message content on the wrist. The estimated glucose, uric acid and lipid readings are not clinically valid and could mislead. ECG certification is not specified, and SpO2 is not listed. There is no GPS, and it is unclear how workouts start without a screen. Battery life of up to 10 days trails the TrackFit’s 30 days at the same price. Weight and the companion app are not specified, and the temperature and Google Health features are not explained.
