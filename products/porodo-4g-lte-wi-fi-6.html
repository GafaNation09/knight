Design and Portability

The Porodo 4G LTE Wi-Fi 6 Pocket Router’s pocket-sized form factor makes it genuinely portable for daily carry alongside a laptop, in a work bag, travel backpack, or jacket pocket. The compact design conceals the 10,000mAh battery and 4G LTE router hardware in a package small enough to place on a desk during meetings, in a car cupholder during road trips, and in overhead storage during flights where the router can be switched to power bank mode.

Two colour options, Black and White, provide professional neutral choices that suit all work and travel environments. The Black variant is the conventional corporate and professional choice, while White provides a cleaner, more modern aesthetic that distinguishes the device in a meeting room or desk setup.

The Nano SIM slot accepts the smallest standard SIM form factor, compatible with all current Kenyan mobile network SIM cards from Safaricom, Airtel, Telkom, and other operators, enabling instant 4G LTE connectivity with any active Nano SIM without adapter plates.

4G LTE Connectivity

The supported 4G frequency bands cover the FDD bands B1, B3, B7, B8, and B20, alongside TDD bands B38 and B40, and WCDMA bands B1 and B8. For Kenya’s mobile networks, the FDD B3 band covers Safaricom and Airtel’s primary 4G LTE frequencies, and the TDD B40 band covers additional 4G TDD deployments, providing broad multi-operator compatibility for buyers across Kenya’s major cities and networks.

The TDD-LTE speed ceiling of 300Mbps download and 300Mbps upload represents the theoretical maximum in ideal network conditions, with real-world speeds dependent on local network coverage, congestion, and signal strength at the location of use. The FDD-LTE 150Mbps download provides the standard 4G speed ceiling that covers smooth 4K streaming, large file downloads, video conferencing, and simultaneous multi-device browsing without bottlenecking for most everyday connectivity scenarios.

WCDMA support maintains connectivity on 3G networks as a fallback in areas where 4G LTE coverage is unavailable, providing continued internet access in rural and lower-coverage areas of Kenya where 4G signal is intermittent.

Wi-Fi 6 and Multi-Device Performance

Wi-Fi 6 on the 2.4GHz band is the most advanced wireless standard the router supports, providing OFDMA multi-user simultaneous data transmission that older Wi-Fi 4 and Wi-Fi 5 routers do not offer. OFDMA allows the router to transmit data to multiple connected devices simultaneously rather than sequentially, improving efficiency and reducing latency when 10 to 16 devices are connected and actively transferring data.

For a team of four to eight remote workers each with a laptop and smartphone connected, the Wi-Fi 6 OFDMA management provides better-sustained throughput per device than a Wi-Fi 5 router managing the same number of simultaneous connections. The 16-device support ceiling covers most small team and family use scenarios where the router serves as the primary internet access point for a temporary workspace, hotel room, outdoor event, or vehicle-based mobile office.

The secure encryption protects the Wi-Fi network from unauthorised access, ensuring that only users with the network password can connect to the router and preventing data interception on the shared 4G LTE connection.

Power Bank Functionality

The 10,000mAh battery serves dual duty as both the router’s power source and a 20W USB-C power bank for charging connected devices. The 20W USB-C output supports fast charging for compatible smartphones including iPhones with MagSafe, Samsung Galaxy phones with 25W charging, and most USB-C fast charge compatible Android phones at up to 20W.

The router operates and provides power bank output simultaneously, meaning buyers can charge a smartphone from the USB-C port while the 4G LTE router shares the internet connection with up to 16 devices, all from the same 10,000mAh battery. The 18W USB-C input recharges the router’s battery from a compatible USB-C wall adapter or laptop USB-C port, with the 18W input speed providing reasonable recharge times between sessions.

Use Cases in Kenya

Remote work and co-working: For professionals who work from locations without reliable Wi-Fi in Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu, and other Kenyan cities including co-working spaces, cafes, and client offices, the router provides a personal 4G LTE hotspot that bypasses unreliable venue Wi-Fi with the security advantage of a personal encrypted network.

Field work and site visits: For engineers, surveyors, and field teams who work at construction sites, agricultural locations, and industrial facilities where Wi-Fi infrastructure does not exist, the router provides 4G LTE connectivity for uploading field data, accessing cloud applications, and maintaining communication with the office.

Travel and transit: For buyers who travel frequently between Kenyan cities by road or visit locations without fixed internet access, the router provides consistent 4G LTE connectivity throughout the journey and at the destination, with the power bank reserve covering device charging during long-distance travel.

Event connectivity: For small outdoor events, pop-up stalls, market trading, and temporary commercial setups where fixed internet is unavailable, the router provides up to 16-device connectivity and 26-hour operating time for a full event day without power access.

Pros and Cons

The Porodo 4G LTE Wi-Fi 6 Pocket Router has clear strengths at KSh 7,000. It delivers 4G LTE with TDD-LTE at 300Mbps and FDD-LTE at 150Mbps download, Wi-Fi 6 OFDMA for up to 16 simultaneous devices, a 10,000mAh battery at 26-hour operating time and 15-day standby, 20W USB-C power bank output for device charging while routing, 18W USB-C input for fast self-recharge, FDD bands B1/3/7/8/20 and TDD bands B38/40 for broad Kenyan operator compatibility, WCDMA 3G fallback, secure encryption, and Black and White colour options at KSh 7,000.

Its main limitations are the single 2.4GHz Wi-Fi band without 5GHz which would provide faster per-device speeds for close-range connected devices, the 15W maximum total output that limits simultaneous charging of multiple devices from the power bank port, the 4G LTE ceiling without 5G for buyers in 5G coverage areas who want 5G speeds, and the performance being dependent on local network conditions and operator coverage in the area of use.
