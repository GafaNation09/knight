Design and Build Quality

The iPhone 15 introduces Dynamic Island to the standard iPhone lineup for the first time — the pill-shaped interactive cutout that replaces the static notch, housing the Face ID sensors and front camera while serving as a live notification hub for music playback, timers, navigation directions, calls, and other system activities. Dynamic Island makes the space around the front camera functional and interactive rather than simply empty, and its absence on the previous iPhone 14 standard model is one of the most immediately noticeable differences when switching.

At 147.6 x 71.6 x 7.8mm and 171 grams, the iPhone 15 is one of the most compact, lightweight flagship smartphones available in Kenya. For buyers who find large phones uncomfortable for single-handed use or prefer a phone that disappears into a pocket, the 6.1-inch iPhone 15 is the right size choice in Apple’s current lineup. The aluminium frame keeps weight low while providing a solid, premium feel, and the five colour options — Black, Blue, Green, Yellow, and Pink — are cheerful and expressive, significantly more personality-driven than the muted titanium tones of the Pro models.

Ceramic Shield glass on the front provides the same drop protection found on the more expensive Pro models, and IP68 waterproofing to 6 metres for 30 minutes matches the Pro’s rating — an unusually deep water resistance certification that goes well beyond the 1.5-metre ratings found on most competing Android phones at this price in Kenya. USB-C replaces Lightning for the first time on a standard iPhone, bringing compatibility with the universal cable standard and DisplayPort output for connecting to external displays.

Display Quality

The 6.1-inch Super Retina XDR OLED display at 1179 x 2556 pixels and 461 ppi is sharp, vivid, and colour-accurate. At 461 ppi it is among the sharpest displays available at this screen size on any smartphone, and the OLED panel’s individual pixel illumination delivers deep blacks and excellent contrast that IPS LCD displays cannot match.

The 2000 nits peak brightness ensures outdoor readability in Kenya’s bright sunlight, with independent testing measuring 1643 nits in real-world conditions — a strong result that places it well ahead of most competing flagship screens at this price. Dolby Vision HDR support ensures that streaming content is displayed with the richest possible colour and contrast on compatible platforms.

The standard iPhone 15 does not include the 120Hz ProMotion adaptive refresh found on the Pro models, running at a standard 60Hz. For everyday use including social media, browsing, calls, and productivity tasks, 60Hz is entirely adequate. Buyers who specifically want 120Hz smooth scrolling should consider the Pro models instead.

Dynamic Island adds a layer of interaction to the top of the display that improves everyday usability — Live Activities and background app status are visible at a glance without needing to open apps or check the notification shade, which becomes a natural part of how the phone communicates information throughout the day.

Camera Performance

Rear Camera System

The 48MP main camera is the biggest camera upgrade in the standard iPhone 15’s history compared to its predecessor. Previous standard iPhone models topped out at 12MP — the iPhone 15’s 48MP sensor on a 1/1.56-inch sensor captures four times more image data and enables 2x optical quality zoom by cropping to the central 12MP of the sensor, effectively giving the iPhone 15 two focal lengths — 26mm and 52mm — from a single lens.

The wide f/1.6 aperture is the widest available on any iPhone at this generation, allowing more light into the sensor than the f/1.78 aperture of the iPhone 14 standard model. Sensor-shift OIS moves the sensor to compensate for hand movement, delivering sharper stills in challenging conditions and smoother video during handheld recording. Dual-pixel PDAF ensures fast, accurate focus across the full frame.

The photonic engine — Apple’s computational imaging pipeline — processes both the RAW sensor data and the final output to produce natural, detailed, and well-exposed images across a wide range of shooting conditions. Dolby Vision HDR video at 4K 60fps is outstanding for a standard-tier iPhone, and Action mode provides heavily stabilised video for active shooting scenarios like sport, walking, and travel footage.

The 12MP ultrawide covers 120 degrees and handles wide scenes, group shots, architecture, and landscape photography. While it lacks the autofocus found on the Pro’s ultrawide, it covers the everyday wide-angle photography needs of most iPhone 15 buyers comfortably.

Selfie Camera

The 12MP front camera with f/1.9 aperture and PDAF records 4K video at up to 60fps with Dolby Vision HDR. The SL 3D depth sensor powers Face ID and Portrait Mode on the front camera. The wide f/1.9 aperture and large sensor for a front camera deliver well-exposed, sharp selfies in a range of lighting conditions, and gyro-EIS keeps video stable during recording.

Performance and Software

The Apple A16 Bionic on a 4nm process was Apple’s Pro chip generation when it launched on the iPhone 14 Pro, and it remains a fast, efficient, and capable processor in 2026. The hexa-core CPU handles every everyday task — social media, streaming, navigation, photography, gaming, and multitasking — with smooth, immediate responsiveness. The 5-core GPU manages graphically demanding games and creative apps without lag.

With an AnTuTu v10 score of 1,354,651 and GeekBench v6 single-core of 6,526, the A16 Bionic comfortably outperforms most competing Android chipsets at the same price point in Kenya, delivering a performance level that remains relevant for the full expected lifespan of the phone. GFXBench confirms a sustained 60fps gaming result, confirming the GPU holds up under extended gaming sessions without throttling.

iOS 26.5 support means the iPhone 15 has already benefited from multiple major feature updates since its 2023 launch and will continue to receive support for several more years. Apple’s iOS update record for standard iPhone models typically runs to five or six years from launch, placing the iPhone 15’s end-of-support window around 2028 to 2029. This long-term software commitment provides genuine future-proofing for buyers in Kenya who plan to keep their phone for several years.

Ultra Wideband gen2 enables precise spatial awareness for AirDrop and AirTag tracking. Emergency SOS and Find My via satellite provide safety connectivity outside cellular coverage areas — a meaningful practical feature for users who travel to rural areas of Kenya. Apple Pay via NFC enables contactless payments at supported terminals.

Battery Life and Charging

The 3349mAh battery delivers 13 hours 20 minutes of active use in independent testing and an endurance rating of 98 hours — respectable results for a compact flagship with a high-resolution OLED display and a powerful chip. In real-world moderate use, a full working day of calls, social media, navigation, and streaming is comfortably achievable, though heavy users may find themselves reaching for a charger by early evening.

MagSafe 15W wireless charging aligns magnetically to the back of the phone for optimal charging position without fiddling with cable placement. Qi2 wireless charging at 15W is supported from iOS 17.2 onwards, opening compatibility to the growing ecosystem of third-party Qi2 chargers. Wired PD2.0 charging reaches 50 percent in 30 minutes with a 20W or higher USB-C power adapter, and the 4.5W reverse wired charging can power Apple Watch through the USB-C port.

Pros and Cons

The Apple iPhone 15 has clear and meaningful strengths. It offers a 48MP main camera on a 1/1.56-inch sensor with sensor-shift OIS, 4K Dolby Vision HDR video, a 6.1-inch Super Retina XDR OLED at 461 ppi, Dynamic Island, Ceramic Shield glass, IP68 waterproofing to 6 metres, the A16 Bionic chip, Face ID, MagSafe 15W wireless charging, USB-C with DisplayPort, Ultra Wideband gen2, Emergency SOS via satellite, Apple Pay, and iOS upgradable to iOS 26.5 — in a compact 171-gram body at KES 89,000.

Its main limitations are the 60Hz display refresh rate versus the 120Hz ProMotion on Pro models, a 3349mAh battery that is smaller than most competing Android phones at this price, no ProRes video recording, no telephoto zoom lens, USB-C 2.0 rather than the 3.2 Gen 2 on the Pro Max, 6GB RAM versus 8GB on the Pro, and no LiDAR scanner.
