Samsung Galaxy A18 4G Price in Kenya, Full Specifications and Review (2026)

The Samsung Galaxy A18 4G price in Kenya starts at KSh 26,000. Released on 18 September 2026, it is Samsung’s newest budget Galaxy A phone. It pairs a 6.7-inch Super AMOLED 90Hz display and the Exynos 1610 chipset with a triple camera system led by a 50MP main sensor. It has a 5,000mAh battery with 25W charging and IP64 dust and water resistance. It ships with Android 17 and One UI 9, and Samsung promises up to six major OS updates.

The headline here is software support. Six major Android updates from Android 17 means the A18 should still receive new Android versions well into the next decade, which is rare in this price class. Most budget phones get two or three. For buyers in Kenya who keep phones for years, it means continued security patches for M-Pesa and banking apps, new features and better resale value. The trade-offs are 4GB of RAM on every variant and 4G-only connectivity at a time when 5G is spreading.

The comparison at Phonesstore Kenya is the Samsung Galaxy A17 4G at KSh 24,000 for 4GB/128GB, its predecessor. The A18 costs KSh 2,000 more. For that, it adds an ultrawide camera, a faster Exynos 1610 chip, stronger IP64 protection, Gorilla Glass 3, a slimmer and lighter body and a newer Android version. The A17 4G remains the cheaper option and offers up to 8GB of RAM.

You can buy the Samsung Galaxy A18 4G from Phonesstore Kenya, where it is available with competitive pricing and nationwide delivery across Kenya.

Design and Durability

At 7.7mm thick and 196g, the Galaxy A18 4G is slim and light for a phone with a 6.7-inch screen and a 5,000mAh battery. It is slightly thinner and lighter than the A17 4G. The back and frame are plastic, which is normal at this price and less prone to shattering than glass. The front uses Corning Gorilla Glass 3. That is an older generation of Gorilla Glass, but still better than the untreated glass on many budget phones, so a screen protector is still a good idea.

The IP64 rating means the phone is fully dust-tight and protected against water splashes from any direction. The “6” for dust is a step up from the A17’s IP54, which only offers partial dust protection. For Kenyan conditions, such as dusty roads, construction sites or a phone carried on a boda boda, full dust protection helps keep grit out of ports and buttons. Splash protection covers rain and spills, but the phone isn’t built for submersion.

Display

The 6.7-inch Super AMOLED panel is one of the A18’s strongest features. AMOLED pixels produce their own light, so blacks are truly black and colours look vivid, a clear advantage over the LCD screens common on rivals at this price. Full HD+ resolution (1080 x 2340) keeps text and video sharp, and the 90Hz refresh rate makes scrolling smoother than a standard 60Hz screen.

Measured peak brightness was 775 nits. That is adequate for outdoor use, though under direct midday sun the screen will be harder to read than on pricier phones that reach 1,500 nits or more.

Performance

The Exynos 1610 is a clear step up for Samsung’s budget line. It combines two faster Cortex-A7X performance cores at 2.4GHz with six efficiency cores. Tested scores of 483,061 on AnTuTu v10 and 2,144 on Geekbench 6 put it comfortably ahead of typical entry-level chips. It handles WhatsApp, M-Pesa, social media, YouTube and casual games smoothly. Its graphics score of 384 in 3DMark Wild Life Extreme means demanding 3D games will run, but at lower settings.

The main limitation is memory. Every variant has 4GB of RAM, which is enough for everyday use but can mean apps reload when switching between many of them, and it may feel tighter in later years as Android updates add features. Buyers should also weigh the base 64GB storage. Android and Samsung’s apps take a significant share of it, so the 128GB or 256GB versions are the safer choice. A microSD card adds space, but it shares the second SIM slot, so users must choose between dual SIM and expanded storage.

Software and Updates

The A18 ships with Android 17 and One UI 9, and Samsung promises up to six major OS updates. That means Android versions all the way up to Android 23. Long update support is the A18’s biggest advantage over most competitors in this price range. It keeps security patches flowing, keeps banking and M-Pesa apps compatible for longer, adds new features over time and helps resale value. For buyers planning to keep their phone for four years or more, it is a strong reason to choose Samsung over cheaper brands with shorter support.

Cameras

The rear camera system is led by a 50MP main sensor with an f/1.8 aperture and phase-detection autofocus. In good light, it produces detailed, well-exposed photos, with Samsung’s typically vivid processing. The A18 adds a 5MP ultrawide camera, which the A17 4G lacks, for fitting in group photos, buildings and landscapes. Its low resolution means ultrawide shots will look softer than the main camera’s, especially in dim light. The 2MP macro camera is of limited practical use.

The 13MP selfie camera handles video calls and social media well. Video tops out at 1080p and 30fps on both front and rear cameras, with no 4K or 60fps. That is a clear limitation for content creators, but fine for casual clips.

Battery and Charging

The 5,000mAh battery earned an active use score of 13 hours 10 minutes in testing. That comfortably gets most users through a full day of mixed use, with heavy users finishing the day with some charge left. Charging peaks at 25W, reaching about 50% in 30 minutes. That is slower than many Chinese-brand rivals offering 33W to 45W at similar prices, but acceptable. A 25W Samsung charger is sold separately; Phonesstore Kenya stocks the Samsung 25W Fast Charger.

Connectivity

The A18 is a 4G phone with no 5G support. Safaricom’s and Airtel’s 4G networks cover most populated areas of Kenya well, and 4G speeds are enough for streaming, video calls and social media. Buyers who want to use 5G as networks expand will need a 5G model, such as the Galaxy A17 5G. Other connectivity includes dual-band Wi-Fi 5, Bluetooth 5.3, NFC (depending on market) and USB-C 2.0. There’s no 3.5mm headphone jack, so wired headphones need a USB-C adapter or USB-C earphones.

Who Is the Samsung Galaxy A18 4G For?

Ideal for:

  • Buyers who want a phone that will receive Android updates for many years
  • Budget-conscious users who want a quality AMOLED display
  • Students and young professionals who want a reliable Samsung for WhatsApp, M-Pesa, social media and streaming
  • Users in dusty environments who benefit from IP64 dust protection

Not ideal for:

  • Buyers who want 5G
  • Heavy multitaskers and gamers who need more than 4GB of RAM
  • Content creators who want 4K video
  • Buyers who need fast charging above 25W

Pros and Cons

The Samsung Galaxy A18 4G has genuine strengths from KSh 26,000. Up to six major OS updates from Android 17 means exceptional long-term support. Its 6.7-inch Super AMOLED 90Hz display is vivid. The Exynos 1610 chip performs well (AnTuTu v10: 483,061). It adds an ultrawide camera to the 50MP main sensor, IP64 dust-tight and splash resistance, and Gorilla Glass 3. It is slim and light at 7.7mm and 196g, and the 5,000mAh battery scored 13h 10m of active use. It also offers microSD expansion and NFC in supported markets.

Its main limitations are 4G only, 4GB of RAM on all variants, and a 64GB base storage that fills quickly. Charging is 25W, and video tops out at 1080p and 30fps. The 5MP ultrawide is low resolution, and Gorilla Glass 3 is an older generation. There’s no 3.5mm headphone jack, and stereo speakers are not confirmed.

Samsung Galaxy A18 4G vs Samsung Galaxy A17 4G

The A18 4G is the A17 4G’s successor, and the upgrades are meaningful. The A18, from KSh 26,000, moves to the faster Exynos 1610 chip from the A17’s MediaTek Helio G99. It adds a 5MP ultrawide camera and upgrades dust protection from IP54 to IP64. It adds Gorilla Glass 3, ships with Android 17 and is slightly slimmer and lighter. The A17 4G, from KSh 24,000 for 4GB/128GB, is KSh 2,000 cheaper. It offers RAM options up to 8GB and stereo speakers according to its store listing. Both have 6.7-inch Super AMOLED 90Hz displays, 50MP main cameras, 5,000mAh batteries, 25W charging and up to six OS updates.
