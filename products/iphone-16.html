Design and Build Quality

The iPhone 16 shares its 147.6 x 71.6 x 7.8mm dimensions with the iPhone 15, but Apple has made meaningful internal changes that affect the everyday experience. At 170 grams — one gram lighter than the iPhone 15 — it remains one of the lightest flagship smartphones available in Kenya, and the compact 6.1-inch form factor continues to make it the right choice for buyers who prioritise single-handed use and pocket-friendliness over screen size.

The 2024 generation Ceramic Shield glass is Apple’s most improved iteration of the proprietary fortified glass, offering better drop protection than the version used on the iPhone 15. This directly addresses one of the most common everyday risks for smartphone users, giving iPhone 16 buyers improved confidence in daily handling without a case.

The five colour options — Black, White, Pink, Teal, and Ultramarine — are expressive and distinctive, continuing Apple’s move toward bolder, more memorable colours on the standard iPhone line while offering the understated Black and White for buyers who prefer classic finishes. Ultramarine is a standout option — a rich, saturated blue that photographs beautifully and stands out in a pocket full of dark-coloured devices.

A significant new hardware addition is the Camera Control button, located on the right side of the phone. It provides a dedicated physical button for accessing and controlling the camera — a single press opens the camera from any screen, a swipe scrolls through camera modes, and applying pressure adjusts parameters like zoom and exposure. For buyers who use the camera frequently throughout the day, the Camera Control reduces the friction of pulling out the phone and navigating to the camera app.

IP68 waterproofing to 6 metres for 30 minutes matches the iPhone 15 Pro’s rating and provides the deepest water resistance available on a standard iPhone, significantly exceeding the 1.5-metre ratings common on competing Android phones at this price.

Display Quality

The 6.1-inch Super Retina XDR OLED display at 1179 x 2556 pixels and 460 ppi is visually identical to the iPhone 15’s display in specifications, but the 2024 Ceramic Shield glass adds improved drop protection without changing the viewing experience. Independent testing measured 1708 nits in real-world conditions, confirming strong outdoor readability for Kenya’s bright sunlight.

The 2000 nits peak brightness ensures the display remains clearly legible under direct midday sun, and Dolby Vision HDR support delivers rich, accurate colour for compatible streaming content on Netflix, Apple TV+, YouTube, and other platforms. The OLED panel’s true black and high contrast ratio adds depth and visual quality that IPS LCD displays cannot achieve.

The iPhone 16 standard model retains the 60Hz display of the iPhone 15 — the 120Hz ProMotion adaptive refresh remains exclusive to the Pro models in Apple’s lineup. For everyday social media, messaging, productivity, and video consumption, 60Hz is comfortable and functional. Buyers who specifically want 120Hz smooth scrolling and gaming should consider the Pro models.

Dynamic Island is carried over from the iPhone 15, providing the interactive front cutout that displays Live Activities for music, timers, calls, navigation, and other background processes as a living part of the display interface.

Camera Performance

Rear Camera System

The iPhone 16’s camera hardware is closely related to the iPhone 15’s, but two meaningful improvements distinguish it. The ultrawide camera gains dual-pixel PDAF — autofocus capability that was absent on the iPhone 15’s ultrawide — enabling sharp close-focus macro photography and more reliable results on subjects at varying distances from the lens. This makes the iPhone 16’s camera system more versatile for everyday photography than its predecessor.

The A18 chip’s more powerful neural engine also enables camera features that required the Pro chip in previous generations. Most significantly, the iPhone 16 gains support for Apple Intelligence photography features including Clean Up — an AI tool that removes unwanted objects from photos — and Photo Styles that apply real-time processing adjustments to camera output with greater intelligence and flexibility than the previous generation’s filters.

The 48MP primary camera with f/1.6 aperture, sensor-shift OIS, and dual-pixel PDAF is carried over from the iPhone 15, delivering the same strong daylight and low-light performance on the large 1/1.56-inch sensor. The wide f/1.6 aperture is the widest available on any iPhone at this generation, capturing more light than competing f/1.7 and f/1.8 apertures for better results in challenging lighting conditions. The photonic engine processes both RAW sensor data and final output for consistently natural, detailed results.

4K Dolby Vision HDR video at up to 60fps produces colour-accurate, cinema-quality footage. Spatial audio recording on the front camera captures three-dimensional sound that plays back on compatible speakers and AirPods.

Selfie Camera

The 12MP front camera with f/1.9 aperture, PDAF, and SL 3D depth sensor records 4K video at up to 60fps with Dolby Vision HDR and spatial audio. PDAF ensures sharp focus at varying selfie distances, and the SL 3D sensor powers Face ID biometric authentication and Portrait Mode with accurate subject separation. gyro-EIS keeps front-facing video stable during handheld recording.

Performance and Apple Intelligence

The Apple A18 on a 3nm process is a significant step forward from the A16 Bionic in the iPhone 15, and — crucially — it is the chip that enables Apple Intelligence on a standard iPhone for the first time. With an AnTuTu v10 score of 1,721,149 and GeekBench v6 of 7,929, the A18 delivers a performance level that confidently outperforms virtually every competing Android chipset at this price in Kenya and handles every everyday task with immediate, lag-free responsiveness.

Apple Intelligence is the most consequential new feature of the iPhone 16 generation for buyers in Kenya. Writing Tools rewrite, proofread, and summarise text across virtually every app with a single tap. Image Playground generates custom images from text descriptions. Genmoji creates personalised emoji from descriptions. Enhanced Siri understands context across apps, can take actions within applications, and integrates with third-party services. Notification summaries group and condense alerts so you see the essential information at a glance. Priority Messages in Mail surfaces the most important emails automatically. Clean Up removes unwanted objects from photos using on-device AI. All of this processing happens on the A18’s neural engine — your data stays on the device.

The 8GB RAM — up from 6GB on the iPhone 15 — provides the memory headroom that Apple Intelligence features require, and enables more demanding multitasking without app refreshes when switching between open applications. With NVMe storage, the phone launches apps instantly and handles large file access quickly.

iOS 18 upgradable to iOS 26.5 means the iPhone 16 has already received multiple major feature updates and will continue to receive iOS support for several more years — Apple’s support track record for standard iPhones typically extends to five or six years of major iOS updates from launch.

Camera Control adds a dedicated hardware camera button that integrates with iOS and third-party apps, giving photographers tactile, physical access to the camera system throughout the day. It also enables visual intelligence features where pointing the camera at objects, restaurants, or text and pressing Camera Control surfaces relevant information through Apple Intelligence.

Battery Life and Charging

The 3561mAh battery is larger than the iPhone 15’s 3349mAh cell and delivers noticeably better battery performance — 15 hours 42 minutes of active use in independent testing compared to the iPhone 15’s 13 hours 20 minutes, a meaningful improvement. For moderate users, the iPhone 16 comfortably covers a full working day of calls, messaging, photography, navigation, and streaming.

The headline battery upgrade over the iPhone 15 is 25W MagSafe wireless charging, reaching 50 percent in 30 minutes — up from 15W MagSafe on the previous generation. This makes wireless charging a genuinely fast and convenient primary charging method for the first time on a standard iPhone. Qi2 wireless charging at 25W works with compatible third-party accessories. Wired PD2.0 charging also reaches 50 percent in 30 minutes with a compatible 20W or higher USB-C adapter. The 4.5W reverse wired charging powers Apple Watch through the USB-C port.

Pros and Cons

The Apple iPhone 16 has clear and meaningful strengths. It delivers full Apple Intelligence support, the A18 3nm chip with AnTuTu over 1.7 million, Camera Control hardware button, 25W MagSafe wireless charging at 50% in 30 minutes, a 48MP camera with f/1.6 aperture and OIS, an ultrawide with dual-pixel autofocus, 4K Dolby Vision HDR video, 8GB RAM, the latest Ceramic Shield glass, IP68 waterproofing to 6 metres, Face ID, Wi-Fi 7, Ultra Wideband gen2, Emergency SOS via satellite, Apple Pay, and iOS upgradable to iOS 26.5 — in a 170-gram compact body at KES 104,000.

Its main limitations are the 60Hz display refresh rate versus the 120Hz ProMotion on Pro models, a 3561mAh battery that while improved is smaller than most competing Android phones at this price, USB-C 2.0 rather than the 3.2 Gen 2 on the Pro models, no telephoto zoom lens, no ProRes video recording, and no microSD card or 3.5mm headphone jack.
